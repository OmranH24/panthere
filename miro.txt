Nice to meet you, Panthère.

I met you there induring the season I was playing there.

There was this sunset party by the sea, one of those high-end gatherings full of successful people, business conversations, fake smiles, and people trying very hard to look important. I looked like I belonged there, dressed well, confident, all of that… but honestly, that whole scene was never really my vibe.

I noticed you before we spoke. Not just because you were beautiful, but because guys kept coming up to you all night and you kept politely brushing them off like you’d done it a hundred times before.

At some point I’m standing near you at the bar waiting for my water, I see another guy walking straight toward you, look at you with a smirk and go:

“Here comes another one.”

You look at me like, “What?”

I nod toward him.

“What is he, number six tonight?”

You laugh and ask if I’ve been counting.

“Hard not to notice. You reject men like it’s part of your evening routine.”

Then you give me that look like maybe I’m about to become number seven.

So I tell you:

“Relax. I’m not here for you. I just need my water and I’m heading out.”

“Heading out where?”

“Going for a cruise and a sesh.”

You look at me like I just broke character.

“What the fuck?”

I just look at you.

“What?”

And when you keep staring:

“None of your business anyway.”

Then I grab my water and actually start walking away.

You stop me and go, “Wait… you’re seriously leaving this party to drive around and smoke?”

“That’s the plan.”

And then you say something I genuinely don’t expect:

“That sounds better than this.”

That’s probably the first moment you really surprise me.

Because five minutes earlier, I had you figured out as this very composed, disciplined, successful woman who belonged perfectly in that world.

So I look at you for a second and go:

“You?”

And somehow, twenty minutes later, we’re driving along the coast with the windows down, music on, no real destination.

At some point I pull out a joint, still half expecting you to judge me.

Instead, you take it from my hand.

And that’s when I realize the woman I was watching at that party and the woman sitting beside me are probably two very different people.

I think that’s where Miro actually starts getting curious about Panthère.
